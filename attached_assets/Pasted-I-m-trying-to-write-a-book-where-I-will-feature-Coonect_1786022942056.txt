This is a genuinely smart strategy. A few honest thoughts:

What's working well

The concept is inherently stronger than traditional marketing because you're leading with value — readers buy the book for the ideas, and Coonected earns credibility by appearing as a real-world proof point rather than an ad. The author bio sets this up well: it frames Coonected as the result of a philosophy, not a product pitch.

The frameworks (Opportunity Lifecycle, Flywheel, Compass) are your biggest asset. If those become the language people use to talk about opportunity, Coonected gets permanently associated with that vocabulary. Think "Blue Ocean Strategy" → the consultancy behind it.

The integration risk to avoid

The Coonected mentions need to feel discovered by readers, not inserted. The best case study books (Built to Last, Zero to One, The Lean Startup) let the example make the principle vivid — the product fades into the background. If Coonected appears in chapters where it's a natural fit, readers won't notice it's your platform. If it appears in chapters where it's a stretch, it poisons the whole book.

Where Coonected fits naturally (and where it doesn't)

Strong fits — the connection is organic:

    Chapter 10 (Engineering Opportunity / Building platforms at scale) — the actual story of building Coonected
    Chapter 11 (Opportunity through relationships — networks, mentorship, collaboration) — directly maps to the platform's features
    Chapter 20 (Building Opportunity Systems — communities, platforms, networks) — Coonected as a designed system
    Chapter 29 (AI and the Opportunity Explosion) — the AI Twin, AI agent, opportunity matcher
    Chapter 30 (Creating Opportunity at Scale) — the global mission angle

Weaker fits where you'd want real third-party examples instead:

    Psychology chapters (1–4) — these need universal human stories, not tech platforms
    Risk/evaluation chapters (13–15) — better served by investment or business case studies

One structural suggestion

Consider a recurring sidebar format — something like "In Practice: Coonected" — a short box at the end of relevant chapters showing how that chapter's principle was implemented in the platform. This is honest (you're the author, transparency is good), keeps the main narrative clean, and gives readers a concrete takeaway. It also makes the platform feel like a living example rather than a retrospective case study.

The promotional mechanics

The book gives you several things money can't buy: speaking invitations, media coverage, partnership conversations with universities and companies, and a reason for anyone who reads it to visit the platform. If you add a "Reader's companion" at coonected.com — where readers can apply the frameworks using the platform's tools — the book becomes an onboarding funnel.

Bottom line

The concept is solid and the timing is right — AI and opportunity are genuinely top of mind globally. The main execution risk is letting the Coonected mentions drift into self-promotion. Keep the platform as the illustration, make the frameworks the star, and the book will do more for the platform than any ad campaign could.
