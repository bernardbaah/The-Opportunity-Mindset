# Appendix C: The Opportunity Skills Framework

*The Opportunity Mindset · Bernard Baah · Filly Coder AI Future Series*

---

The Opportunity Skills Framework is a layered competency model describing how capability develops across the five core skill domains of an Opportunity Mindset. Each domain progresses through five levels: Beginner, Developing, Proficient, Advanced, and Expert.

For each level, the framework describes:
- **What someone at this level can do** (behavioral indicators)
- **What they need to develop next** (the critical gap to the next level)
- **How to progress** (specific, actionable development moves)

Use this framework to locate yourself honestly in each domain, then focus development energy on the gaps most relevant to your current opportunity context.

---

## Domain 1: Opportunity Recognition

*The ability to detect signals of unmet need, emerging trends, and exploitable gaps before they become obvious to the general population.*

---

### Level 1 — Beginner

**What they can do:**
- Identifies obvious opportunities after they are pointed out by others
- Notices when something doesn't work but cannot articulate why
- Responds to clearly stated problems when someone else frames them
- Reads and absorbs trend information when exposed to it passively

**What they need to develop:**
- A habit of active scanning rather than passive reception
- The vocabulary to describe what they notice in structured terms
- Comfort asking "why does this exist?" and "what's missing here?" as default questions

**How to progress:**
- Keep an Opportunity Log: one observation per day about something that seems broken, inefficient, or underserved. Review it weekly.
- Read one article per day from an unfamiliar domain and write one sentence about what problem it's describing.
- Practice the "Five Whys" on a frustration you experience regularly. Push beyond the surface-level explanation.

---

### Level 2 — Developing

**What they can do:**
- Actively scans specific domains for signals of unmet need
- Distinguishes between trends and noise with some reliability
- Frames problems in terms of who is affected, how severely, and how frequently
- Recognizes opportunities in domains where they have direct experience

**What they need to develop:**
- Cross-domain pattern recognition — the ability to see parallels between sectors
- Quantitative framing of opportunity size beyond subjective assessment
- Speed and consistency across diverse contexts, not just familiar ones

**How to progress:**
- Maintain a Trend Map: track 5–10 trends monthly and assess their potential impact on your primary domain.
- Practice describing every problem you notice in this structure: *"[Specific group] struggles with [specific problem] because [root cause], and current solutions fail because [gap]."*
- Read across at least two unrelated industries per month; write one connection between them.

---

### Level 3 — Proficient

**What they can do:**
- Consistently identifies non-obvious opportunities across multiple domains
- Uses structured methods (jobs-to-be-done, signal-to-noise filtering, competitor gap analysis) to surface insights
- Quantifies opportunity size with reasonable accuracy using available data
- Articulates why an opportunity is underserved, not just that it is

**What they need to develop:**
- Ability to recognize opportunities in domains with little prior knowledge
- Developing and testing hypotheses about future opportunity spaces
- Reducing time from signal detection to actionable framing

**How to progress:**
- Conduct one structured opportunity scan per quarter using at least three research methods (user interviews, market data, platform reviews, competitive analysis).
- Build a personal Signal Library: curated sources organized by domain, reviewed on a regular schedule.
- Practice presenting an opportunity thesis — not just the idea but the evidence chain that supports it — to a critical audience.

---

### Level 4 — Advanced

**What they can do:**
- Recognizes opportunities in emerging domains before market consensus
- Constructs original frameworks for opportunity detection tailored to specific industries
- Filters high-quality signals from abundant information with consistent accuracy
- Teaches others to see opportunities in domains the Advanced practitioner knows well

**What they need to develop:**
- Systematic mechanisms for opportunity recognition that operate independent of active attention
- Building institutional memory of past pattern recognition to improve future accuracy
- Calibrating confidence levels to reduce both over-confidence and premature dismissal

**How to progress:**
- Build an automated intelligence system: RSS feeds, Slack channels, and AI-powered research tools organized to surface relevant signals without manual effort.
- Maintain a Recognition Journal tracking predictions made versus outcomes observed. Review quarterly.
- Mentor at least one Developing practitioner through a structured opportunity scan.

---

### Level 5 — Expert

**What they can do:**
- Identifies opportunities in markets, industries, and contexts most others cannot see
- Builds and refines original pattern-recognition systems used by others
- Operates at the intersection of multiple domains, seeing opportunities invisible to specialists
- Creates conditions (communities, platforms, research programs) that systematically surface new opportunities for groups of people

**What they need to develop:**
- Continued calibration of overconfidence risk at this level
- Actively seeking to be challenged and proven wrong
- Documenting and sharing methodology to leverage recognition capability beyond personal application

**How to progress:**
- Publish a structured analysis of an emerging opportunity area, inviting critique from peers and practitioners in adjacent fields.
- Convene a small group of advanced practitioners across different domains for regular opportunity review sessions.
- Build a mentoring or teaching structure that transmits recognition skills to practitioners at Levels 1–3.

## Domain 2: Opportunity Creation

*The ability to generate new opportunities that did not previously exist by combining existing resources, relationships, and ideas in novel configurations.*

---

### Level 1 — Beginner

**What they can do:**
- Generates ideas in response to prompts or structured exercises
- Combines familiar concepts in straightforward ways
- Proposes improvements to existing solutions rather than creating new ones
- Produces volume of ideas when given frameworks and time

**What they need to develop:**
- Moving from idea generation to concept development
- Comfort with ideas that have no existing precedent or obvious market
- Understanding the difference between iteration and invention

**How to progress:**
- Complete a 30-day creativity exercise: generate 10 ideas per day in a specific domain without filtering. Review at day 30 for patterns.
- Study 5 businesses outside your industry and ask: "How could this model apply to [your domain]?"
- Read at least one book in a domain you know nothing about and look for transferable principles.

---

### Level 2 — Developing

**What they can do:**
- Generates original concepts, not just improvements
- Connects observations from multiple domains to form new ideas
- Articulates the value proposition of a new concept before building it
- Moves from idea to rough prototype in low-stakes environments

**What they need to develop:**
- Creating under constraint (time, budget, resources) without quality collapse
- Building a personal creative process that is reliable and repeatable
- Moving faster from concept to visible artifact

**How to progress:**
- Run 72-hour creation challenges: take a concept from idea to shareable prototype in three days.
- Study the creation process of five practitioners you admire. Map their method, not just their output.
- Start a "low-stakes creation" habit: write one short piece, build one small tool, or sketch one concept per week.

---

### Level 3 — Proficient

**What they can do:**
- Creates original products, services, content, or systems that others use and value
- Builds first versions deliberately, knowing iteration will follow
- Designs creation processes that can be sustained over time
- Generates opportunities for others through their own creation activity

**What they need to develop:**
- Scaling creation beyond personal capacity
- Creating systems and templates that others can build on
- Increasing the ambition and scope of what they create

**How to progress:**
- Identify one piece of your creation process that could be systematized and build a template or workflow around it.
- Pursue a creation project one level of ambition above your comfort zone.
- Document your creation process and share it with someone earlier in their development.

---

### Level 4 — Advanced

**What they can do:**
- Creates category-defining work: products, communities, frameworks, or movements that establish new standards
- Runs creation as a system, not just an activity, with consistent output across sustained periods
- Enables and amplifies others' creation through infrastructure, platforms, or mentorship
- Translates abstract insight into concrete, distributable forms others can build on

**What they need to develop:**
- Managing creative scale without losing quality or distinctiveness
- Building institutional structures that sustain creation beyond individual effort
- Navigating the tension between consistency and reinvention

**How to progress:**
- Launch one creation project that explicitly involves enabling others to build or create.
- Study how institutions (labs, studios, schools, companies) sustain creation at scale.
- Identify one aspect of your creation output that has become formulaic and deliberately disrupt it.

---

### Level 5 — Expert

**What they can do:**
- Generates opportunities at scale through platforms, movements, or systems that others participate in and extend
- Creates meta-level frameworks for how creation should happen in a domain
- Produces work that shifts the baseline of what is considered possible
- Inspires, equips, and enables the next generation of creators

**What they need to develop:**
- Maintaining creative vitality over decades, not just years
- Resisting the institutional gravity that turns creation into maintenance
- Remaining genuinely curious in the face of accumulated expertise

**How to progress:**
- Periodically enter a domain where you have no expertise and complete a creation project as a Beginner.
- Build a curriculum, mentorship program, or community structure specifically designed to develop Level 1–3 creators.

## Domain 3: Opportunity Evaluation

*The ability to accurately assess the potential, risk, timing, and fit of an opportunity before committing significant resources.*

---

### Level 1 — Beginner

**What they can do:**
- Distinguishes between a compelling-sounding idea and a viable opportunity when coached
- Identifies obvious risks when prompted
- Understands the concept of market size at a basic level
- Recognizes when an opportunity seems too good to be true

**What they need to develop:**
- Structured evaluation frameworks to replace gut-feel judgment
- Comfort with ambiguity — accepting that perfect information is unavailable
- Understanding the difference between risk and uncertainty

**How to progress:**
- Apply the Opportunity Equation (Appendix B) to 5 opportunities you have recently considered.
- Study one business failure case study per month. Map what warning signs were present but ignored.
- Practice generating a list of reasons why something will *not* work before listing reasons it will.

---

### Level 2 — Developing

**What they can do:**
- Uses structured frameworks to evaluate opportunities systematically
- Identifies key assumptions underlying an opportunity thesis
- Estimates market size and competitive intensity with reasonable accuracy
- Develops clear criteria for what a "go" decision looks like before evaluating

**What they need to develop:**
- Testing assumptions cheaply before committing
- Distinguishing between solvable and unsolvable risks
- Reducing the influence of confirmation bias on evaluation conclusions

**How to progress:**
- Before committing to any opportunity, write your five key assumptions and describe how each could be tested for under $100.
- Conduct a pre-mortem on every significant opportunity: assume it failed and work backward to identify why.
- Have at least one evaluation reviewed by a trusted critic before acting.

---

### Level 3 — Proficient

**What they can do:**
- Runs structured evaluation processes with documented criteria
- Designs and runs quick experiments to test assumptions before full commitment
- Distinguishes between timing risk, execution risk, and market risk
- Accurately predicts which aspects of an opportunity will be hardest

**What they need to develop:**
- Portfolio-level evaluation: assessing how opportunities fit together, not just each in isolation
- Improving accuracy on timing assessments specifically
- Calibrating confidence in judgments against outcomes over time

**How to progress:**
- Maintain an evaluation log: document your assessment of every significant opportunity considered and track outcomes versus predictions.
- Study options theory as applied to business and career decisions.
- Practice explaining an evaluation to someone who will push back hard.

---

### Level 4 — Advanced

**What they can do:**
- Evaluates opportunities at portfolio and system level, not just individually
- Builds evaluation frameworks used by teams and organizations
- Coaches others through rigorous evaluation processes
- Identifies non-obvious risks that most evaluators miss

**What they need to develop:**
- Continued calibration through tracking prediction accuracy over extended periods
- Resisting the pull toward familiar opportunity types and evaluation criteria
- Building evaluation infrastructure that scales beyond personal capacity

**How to progress:**
- Build an evaluation scorecard used consistently across opportunities in your organization or team.
- Review your evaluation track record annually: where were you consistently wrong? What pattern explains it?

---

### Level 5 — Expert

**What they can do:**
- Constructs evaluation systems used across industries or investment classes
- Advises organizations on opportunity portfolio construction and evaluation standards
- Trains others in evaluation methodology
- Accurately assesses opportunity potential in domains where they have minimal prior knowledge

**What they need to develop:**
- Continued epistemic humility — expertise increases the risk of overconfidence
- Actively updating frameworks based on changing conditions rather than defending established approaches

**How to progress:**
- Publish your evaluation methodology and invite critique from peers.
- Mentor practitioners at Levels 2–3 through complex evaluations and document what they learned.

---

## Domain 4: Opportunity Capture

*The ability to move from opportunity identification to committed action — making decisions and taking the first steps with incomplete information and under real constraints.*

---

### Level 1 — Beginner

**What they can do:**
- Takes action when the path is clear and risk is minimal
- Follows a plan when someone else has defined the first steps
- Completes tasks reliably within established structures
- Begins moving when directly encouraged or supported

**What they need to develop:**
- Tolerating ambiguity long enough to take a first step
- Overcoming the perfectionism that delays action pending "enough" information
- Building personal accountability structures independent of external push

**How to progress:**
- Adopt a "72-hour rule": when you identify an opportunity, take at least one concrete action within 72 hours, no matter how small.
- Practice "good enough" decisions: set a decision deadline and commit when you reach it, even without full certainty.
- Track every instance where you delayed an action and identify the specific reason.

---

### Level 2 — Developing

**What they can do:**
- Acts on opportunities without requiring complete certainty
- Defines a minimum viable first step and takes it
- Maintains momentum through early friction and ambiguity
- Builds accountability structures (partners, commitments, deadlines) to sustain follow-through

**What they need to develop:**
- Acting on larger, higher-stakes opportunities with the same decisiveness applied to smaller ones
- Managing energy and motivation across longer execution timelines
- Building systems that reduce reliance on willpower

**How to progress:**
- Complete one 30-day sprint (defined in Appendix B) and debrief what sustained or broke your momentum.
- Identify one opportunity you have been delaying for more than six months. Identify the real reason for delay. Act or consciously decide not to.

---

### Level 3 — Proficient

**What they can do:**
- Captures opportunities reliably across varying conditions and stakes levels
- Builds personal execution systems that function independently of mood and motivation
- Knows when to accelerate and when to pause based on real signals, not anxiety
- Completes what they start at a high rate

**What they need to develop:**
- Capture at higher speed without sacrificing quality
- Managing parallel opportunities without losing focus
- Delegating and distributing capture activities to others

**How to progress:**
- Audit your completion rate: what percentage of projects started in the last year reached their intended outcome?
- Build an execution operating system — a set of consistent daily and weekly practices that structure your capture activity.

---

### Level 4 — Advanced

**What they can do:**
- Captures multiple high-stakes opportunities simultaneously through delegation and systems
- Coaches others through the capture process
- Builds organizational cultures where speed to action is valued and supported
- Converts concepts to committed action within days, not months

**What they need to develop:**
- Avoiding the trap of starting too many opportunities simultaneously
- Sustaining capture energy over multi-year timelines
- Building team capture systems that don't depend on individual heroics

---

### Level 5 — Expert

**What they can do:**
- Builds systems, cultures, and structures that enable others to capture opportunities at scale
- Moves entire organizations from opportunity recognition to committed action rapidly
- Teaches capture as a discipline with repeatable methodology
- Operates at the edge of personal capacity and manages that boundary skillfully

**How to progress:**
- Document and teach your execution methodology.
- Build organizational systems for opportunity capture that outlast your personal involvement.

---

## Domain 5: Opportunity Multiplication

*The ability to leverage initial opportunities to generate multiple subsequent ones — turning singular wins into compounding systems.*

---

### Level 1 — Beginner

**What they can do:**
- Recognizes when one opportunity has opened access to another
- Uses completed work as evidence when pursuing subsequent opportunities
- Asks "what else could this lead to?" after initial outcomes

**What they need to develop:**
- Proactively designing opportunities to have downstream effects
- Building from each win before the momentum dissipates
- Understanding how networks, content, and credibility compound over time

**How to progress:**
- After every project or initiative, ask three questions: Who did I meet? What did I learn? What can I build next from this?
- Study the career trajectory of three practitioners you admire. Map how each opportunity led to the next.

---

### Level 2 — Developing

**What they can do:**
- Designs opportunities with explicit leverage — creating outputs that open further doors
- Builds a public record of work that compounds over time (portfolio, writing, reputation)
- Connects outcomes across domains to generate cross-cutting opportunities

**What they need to develop:**
- Reinvesting more consistently rather than starting fresh after each win
- Building systems (not just habits) that multiply opportunity automatically

---

### Level 3 — Proficient

**What they can do:**
- Runs a self-reinforcing Opportunity Flywheel with minimal external input
- Builds platforms, communities, or content libraries that generate inbound opportunities
- Mentors others as a strategy for their own continued growth

**What they need to develop:**
- Scaling multiplication beyond personal bandwidth
- Building institutions, not just systems

---

### Level 4 — Advanced

**What they can do:**
- Creates opportunity ecosystems that generate value for networks of people, not just themselves
- Builds organizations or platforms that multiply opportunities at scale
- Models multiplication practices for others through teaching and example

---

### Level 5 — Expert

**What they can do:**
- Builds infrastructure that generates opportunity for thousands or millions of people
- Creates lasting systems that multiply opportunity independent of ongoing personal effort
- Leaves behind frameworks, communities, companies, or institutions that outlast their active involvement

**How to progress at any level:** Find someone a level below you. Help them move up. The act of teaching is the most reliable accelerant of mastery across all five domains.

---

*Use this framework as a diagnostic, not a judgment. The goal is not to be at Level 5 in every domain simultaneously — that is rare and unnecessary for most meaningful work. The goal is to know where you are, know where the gaps are, and take deliberate steps to close them.*
