# Appendix D: Opportunity Readiness Self-Assessment

*The Opportunity Mindset · Bernard Baah · Filly Coder AI Future Series*

---

## Instructions

This 50-question assessment measures your current readiness to identify, create, and act on opportunity across seven dimensions. It is not a test — there are no correct answers. Its purpose is to give you an accurate baseline picture of where you are strong, where gaps exist, and which of three development profiles fits your current situation.

**How to complete it:**

Rate each statement on a scale of 1–5:

- **1** = This is rarely or never true for me
- **2** = This is occasionally true for me
- **3** = This is sometimes true for me
- **4** = This is often true for me
- **5** = This is consistently and reliably true for me

Answer based on actual behavior, not aspirations. The assessment is most useful when it reflects reality rather than intention.

**Time required:** 20–30 minutes

**Scoring:** Add your scores for each section and transfer them to the Summary Scorecard at the end.

## Interpretation Guide

### Overall Score Range

**200–250 (80–100%):** You are operating at an advanced level across most dimensions. The greatest risk at this level is coasting on established patterns. Focus on calibration, contribution to others, and pushing into uncomfortable new domains.

**150–199 (60–79%):** You have a solid Opportunity Mindset foundation with meaningful gaps. Identify your two or three lowest-scoring sections and focus development energy there in the next 90 days.

**100–149 (40–59%):** You are in active development. You have real capabilities but they are not yet operating as a consistent system. Prioritize mindset and execution — these are the foundation everything else builds on.

**Below 100 (under 40%):** You are at the beginning of this development arc, which means you have the most to gain. Start with Section 1 (Mindset) and Section 5 (Execution) — these unlock the most in other areas.

---

### Section-Level Interpretation

**Mindset (Section 1):** If below 60%, the frameworks in this book will remain theoretical. Mindset work — therapy, coaching, deliberate exposure to growth environments — is the highest-leverage investment.

**Recognition Skills (Section 2):** If below 60%, you are likely missing opportunities that are visible to others in your environment. A daily Opportunity Log practice is the most direct intervention.

**Creation Capability (Section 3):** If below 60%, you have more ideas than outputs. The gap between knowing and making is closed only by shipping things, however imperfect.

**Evaluation Ability (Section 4):** If below 60%, you may be pursuing low-quality opportunities or failing to act on high-quality ones. Structured evaluation practice — especially pre-mortems — is the most direct intervention.

**Execution Track Record (Section 5):** If below 60%, recognition and creation may be strong but capture is weak. Systems, not intentions, are the fix. Build your personal operating system first.

**Relationship Capital (Section 6):** If below 60%, you are operating with less leverage than available. The investment required to build relationship capital is consistency and generosity over time — there are no shortcuts.

**Legacy Orientation (Section 7):** If below 60%, your opportunity pursuit may be producing results that don't sustain meaning over time. Spend time clarifying what you are ultimately building toward.

## Three Opportunity Profiles

Based on your overall score and section pattern, identify which of these three profiles most closely matches your current state.

---

### Profile 1: The Opportunity Seeker

**Typical overall score:** 80–130 (32–52%)

**Characteristic pattern:** Stronger in Mindset (Section 1) than in Execution (Section 5). Scores in Recognition and Creation may be moderate. Relationship Capital and Legacy Orientation are often low.

**Who they are:** The Opportunity Seeker is motivated, aware, and searching — but the gap between identifying opportunities and capturing them is significant. They often have more ideas than completed projects, more intentions than executed plans. They seek opportunities but have not yet built the systems that generate them.

**Core development priorities:**
1. Build a personal execution operating system — the infrastructure to convert ideas to action consistently
2. Complete one significant project from start to finish before starting the next
3. Invest in one relationship that will challenge and support their growth simultaneously
4. Reduce the volume of opportunities being considered and increase depth on fewer

**What they should do next:** Complete the 30-Day Opportunity Sprint in Appendix B with a single, specific focus.

---

### Profile 2: The Opportunity Builder

**Typical overall score:** 131–185 (52–74%)

**Characteristic pattern:** Stronger in Creation (Section 3) and Execution (Section 5). Recognition and Evaluation are moderate. Relationship Capital and Legacy Orientation vary.

**Who they are:** The Opportunity Builder creates consistently and ships regularly. They have a track record of completion. Their primary gaps are in the quality of opportunity selection (evaluation), the leverage available through relationships, and the scalability of their creation beyond personal capacity.

**Core development priorities:**
1. Upgrade evaluation discipline — develop a more structured process for opportunity selection
2. Invest more deliberately in the quality and diversity of relationships
3. Begin designing opportunities with downstream multiplication in mind
4. Spend more time in domains outside their primary expertise to surface cross-domain opportunities

**What they should do next:** Review the Opportunity Equation (Appendix B, Section 2) for their three most recent completed projects. What does the retrospective scoring reveal about selection quality?

---

### Profile 3: The Opportunity Creator

**Typical overall score:** 186–250 (74–100%)

**Characteristic pattern:** High scores across most sections, with the lowest typically in Legacy Orientation (Section 7) or Relationship Capital breadth.

**Who they are:** The Opportunity Creator generates opportunities not just for themselves but for others. They have strong systems, strong relationships, and strong execution. The primary development frontier at this level is scale — expanding impact beyond personal capacity — and legacy — designing for contribution that outlasts individual involvement.

**Core development priorities:**
1. Identify one system, community, platform, or institution they can build that generates opportunity for others independently of ongoing personal effort
2. Invest in mentoring, teaching, and knowledge-sharing as a primary activity, not a supplement
3. Define a 10-year legacy objective and work backward to current decisions
4. Expand their network into geographies or communities where their impact can have disproportionate effect

**What they should do next:** Review Part 8 of the book (on legacy) and complete the Legacy Mapping exercise with specificity about what they are building and for whom.

## Retaking the Assessment

This assessment is most useful when retaken at regular intervals:

- **First retake:** 90 days after the initial assessment, after a deliberate development sprint
- **Regular cadence:** Every 6 months ongoing

Track changes over time. The goal is not a perfect score but consistent progress across dimensions that matter for the opportunities you are pursuing.

*Scores can decline as your standards rise — that is normal and healthy. An Expert evaluating themselves will often score lower than a Beginner because the Expert is measuring against a higher benchmark.*
