# Appendix B: The Opportunity Mindset Practitioner Guide

*The Opportunity Mindset · Bernard Baah · Filly Coder AI Future Series*

---

This guide is designed for active use, not passive reading. It walks you through a complete application of the book's four core frameworks to a real opportunity you are currently considering or wish to identify. Work through each section with a specific context in mind — a career transition, a business idea, a community initiative, a creative project, or a significant personal goal.

Set aside 60–90 minutes to complete the full guide. You will need something to write with. The goal is not a polished plan but an honest, structured picture of where you are and what your first concrete moves should be.

---

## How to Use This Guide

**Step 1:** Choose a domain. Pick one area of your life where you want to identify or build opportunity: career, business, creative work, education, community, or relationships.

**Step 2:** Work through the four sections in order. Each section builds on the one before it.

**Step 3:** At the end of each section, complete the worksheet. Don't skip these. The worksheets are where the frameworks become real.

**Step 4:** After completing all four sections, write your 30-Day Opportunity Sprint (see the final section).

## Section 1: Opportunity Audit — Where You Are Now

An Opportunity Audit is a structured inventory of your current situation: what assets you have, what gaps exist, and where friction is costing you forward momentum. Most people skip this step and jump directly to goal-setting. That produces plans disconnected from reality. The Audit grounds everything that follows.

### The Four Audit Dimensions

**1. Asset Inventory**

Your opportunity-relevant assets fall into five categories:

- *Knowledge assets*: What do you know well enough to teach? What have you studied, practiced, or accumulated experience in?
- *Skill assets*: What can you do reliably? What have others paid you for or sought your help with?
- *Relationship assets*: Who do you know? Whose trust have you earned? What networks are you inside?
- *Credibility assets*: What have you built, shipped, published, or completed that others can verify?
- *Resource assets*: What do you have access to — time, money, tools, platforms, spaces?

**2. Gap Analysis**

For the domain you chose, identify:

- What capabilities do you need that you do not currently have?
- What relationships are missing from your current network?
- What knowledge do you lack that someone succeeding in this area has?
- What resources would accelerate your progress if you had them?

**3. Friction Map**

Friction is the resistance between where you are and where you want to be. Some friction is external (market conditions, access, systemic barriers). Some is internal (fear, habit, unclear thinking). Both are real; only one is directly within your control. Naming both is important.

- What has stopped you from moving on this opportunity before?
- What conditions would need to change for you to move forward?
- Which of those conditions can you change, and which are you waiting on?

**4. Momentum Audit**

You already have some momentum — small wins, recurring habits, existing relationships, previous attempts. These are easier to build on than starting from zero.

- Where are you already moving in the direction of this opportunity?
- What small actions have you taken, even inconsistently?
- Who is already in your life who cares about or has experience in this domain?

## Section 2: Applying the Opportunity Equation

The Opportunity Equation states:

**Opportunity Value = (Unmet Need × Your Unique Position) ÷ (Risk × Time to Value)**

This is not a mathematical formula — it is a thinking tool. It helps you compare opportunities against each other and assess whether an opportunity is worth pursuing before you invest significant resources.

### Breaking Down the Equation

**Unmet Need**

How large and persistent is the problem or desire this opportunity addresses? A large, persistent, underserved need has higher potential than a small or temporary one. Ask:

- How many people experience this need?
- How much does it bother them or cost them?
- Are current solutions inadequate, too expensive, inaccessible, or unknown to the people who need them?

*Rate: 1 (small/temporary) → 5 (large/persistent/underserved)*

**Your Unique Position**

What makes *you* — specifically — well-placed to pursue this opportunity? This is not a question about being the only person capable. It is about your particular combination of knowledge, relationships, access, and perspective that creates an asymmetric advantage. Ask:

- Do you have inside knowledge of this domain that most people pursuing it lack?
- Do you have relationships that reduce the cost or time to execute?
- Have you experienced the problem personally, giving you empathy and insight competitors lack?
- Are you operating in a geography or community with less competition?

*Rate: 1 (no particular advantage) → 5 (strong unique position)*

**Risk**

What are you wagering on this opportunity? Consider three categories:

- *Capital risk*: How much money could you lose?
- *Time risk*: How much time are you committing, and what is the cost of that time?
- *Reputation risk*: If this fails publicly, what is the reputational exposure?

*Rate: 1 (minimal risk) → 5 (high risk in multiple categories)*

**Time to Value**

How long before the opportunity produces meaningful returns — financial, relational, reputational, or personal? Opportunities with faster feedback cycles are generally easier to sustain motivation through, especially early in your Opportunity Flywheel.

*Rate: 1 (near-term return) → 5 (long time to any return)*

**Applying the Formula:**

Opportunity Value Score = (Unmet Need × Your Unique Position) ÷ (Risk × Time to Value)

A score above 1.5 suggests strong pursuit potential. Between 0.75 and 1.5 suggests moderate potential worth exploring. Below 0.75 suggests either the opportunity needs to be repositioned or you need to improve your position before pursuing it.

## Section 3: Running the Opportunity Compass

The Opportunity Compass is a directional tool that helps you identify *which type* of opportunity is most aligned with your current situation, strengths, and objectives. It has four directional axes:

- **North (Growth):** Opportunities that primarily expand your capabilities, knowledge, or credentials. Best pursued when you are early in a domain or need to close a significant skill gap.
- **South (Income):** Opportunities that primarily generate near-term financial returns. Best pursued when resources are constrained and you need to fund further investments.
- **East (Reach):** Opportunities that primarily expand your network, visibility, and relationships. Best when your ideas or capabilities are ahead of your audience or access.
- **West (Impact):** Opportunities that primarily create value for others, often communities or causes larger than yourself. Best when you have sufficient resources and seek meaning, legacy, or alignment.

Most real opportunities combine multiple directions. The Compass helps you identify the primary direction — which determines how you measure success and where to invest your energy.

### Running the Compass

For your current opportunity, rate how strongly it pulls in each direction on a scale of 1–5:

- North (Growth): Does pursuing this make me significantly more capable?
- South (Income): Does this generate meaningful financial return in the near term?
- East (Reach): Does this expand who knows about my work and what I can do?
- West (Impact): Does this create measurable value for others beyond myself?

Plot your scores. The highest-scoring direction is your primary compass orientation. Decisions about how to pursue this opportunity should be calibrated to that orientation — different metrics of success, different timelines, different resources required.

## Section 4: Building Your Opportunity Flywheel

The Opportunity Flywheel turns your one-time opportunity into a self-reinforcing system. This section helps you design the flywheel for your specific context by defining what each stage looks like for you.

The Flywheel: **Discover → Learn → Build → Connect → Create → Reinvest**

For each stage, you will define: *What this stage looks like for me specifically*, *What action I will take*, and *What I will produce or generate.*
